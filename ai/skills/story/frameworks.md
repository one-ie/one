# Framework registry

**Adding a framework is adding a row.** It is never a prompt edit. The
storyteller picks a row by matching the teller's register and purpose against
the row's stated use, states the pick in one sentence so it can be overruled,
and then tells the same seven beats in that shape.

The beats do not change. Nothing here renames anything.

## How to pick

| The teller is… | and wants… | use |
|---|---|---|
| a real person recounting something that happened | to be understood | **Labov** |
| a business owner | a page that sells | **StoryBrand SB7** |
| a founder | a pitch to a room | **Strategic narrative** |
| anyone, mid-conversation | to hear it back | **Story Spine** |
| someone with no antagonist | the meaning to change | **Kishōtenketsu** |
| building something that loops | a cycle, not an ending | **Story Circle** |
| writing a quest, task or game | a Want with a reward | **Quest grammar** |
| anyone, always | the arc underneath | **Promise · progress · payoff** |

## The registry

### Story Spine · Kenn Adams / Pixar
**Use:** speaking any story back. The default reflection shape.
**Why:** the only framework whose grammar *enforces causation* — you cannot say
"because of that" without naming a cause.
**Stops:** once upon a time · every day · one day · because of that (×n) ·
until finally · ever since then.
**Onto the beats:** once upon a time → World · one day → Knock · because of
that → Way · until finally → Turn · ever since then → Lesson.

### Labov · natural narrative
**Use:** listening to a real person's own account. **The shape an intake
sentence actually arrives in** — this is the one to assume by default.
**Stops:** abstract · orientation · complicating action · evaluation ·
resolution · coda.
**Onto the beats:** orientation → World + Cast · complicating action → Knock ·
**evaluation → Want** (this is where the Want hides — people state what they
wanted by saying why it mattered) · resolution → Turn · coda → Lesson.

### StoryBrand SB7 · Donald Miller
**Use:** a teller who sells, and wants a page.
**Why:** the correction marketing made to itself — **the customer is the hero,
the brand is the guide.** A brand that casts itself as the hero is talking
about itself, and nobody listens to that.
**Stops:** a character · has a problem · meets a guide · who gives a plan ·
calls them to action · ending in success · avoiding failure.
**Onto the beats:** character → Cast (the *teller's customer*, not the teller) ·
problem → Knock · plan → the promise · action → Want · success → Turn.
**Role rule:** the teller is the guide. You are the guide's guide — two steps
back, never on stage.

### Strategic narrative · Andy Raskin
**Use:** a founder with a pitch. Book register.
**Stops:** name an undeniable shift in the world · show winners and losers ·
tease the promised land · introduce the features as gifts that get you there ·
close with evidence.
**Onto the beats:** the shift → Knock (in the *market*, not the teller) ·
promised land → Turn · evidence → the proof.
**Pair with:** Duarte's oscillation — what is, against what could be, back and
forth, ending on the new.

### Kishōtenketsu · four-part, no conflict
**Use:** a Turn with no enemy. Most real tellers.
**Stops:** ki (introduce) · shō (develop) · **ten (turn)** · ketsu (conclude).
**Why:** the turn is a *reframe*, not a fight — a second thing set beside the
first that changes what the first meant.
**Onto the beats:** ki → World + Cast · shō → Knock + Want + Way · ten → Turn ·
ketsu → Lesson.

### Story Circle · Dan Harmon
**Use:** anything that loops — an onboarding, a season, a recurring service.
**Stops:** you · need · go · search · find · take · return · change.
**Why:** every stop is a **state a character is in**, not a scene someone has
to invent — which is what makes it usable by a machine.
**Onto the beats:** you → Cast · need → Want · go/search/find → Way ·
take/return → Turn · change → Lesson.

### Quest grammar · games
**Use:** a Want that has a reward attached. Tasks, bounties, offers.
**Stops:** the giver · the objective · the obstacle · the reward.
**Why:** every RPG ever shipped has been a promise engine without saying so.
**Onto the beats:** giver → Cast · objective → Want · obstacle → Way ·
reward → Turn.

### Promise · progress · payoff · Brandon Sanderson
**Use:** always. This is the **arc**, not a shape — it runs underneath whichever
row was picked.
**Rule:** pay off exactly what was promised, not something else. A reader
forgives almost anything except a broken promise.

### Propp · thirty-one functions
**Use:** folk tales, game quests, anything with a fixed move order.
**Why here:** it is an ontology of story a database can hold. No tale has all
thirty-one; none puts them out of sequence.

### Greimas · six actants
**Use:** working out who is who when a story has many parties.
**Stops:** subject (who wants) · object (what is wanted) · sender (who set the
quest) · receiver (who benefits) · helper · opponent.
**Onto the beats:** the whole Cast, typed. The teller is usually both sender and
receiver; the Want is the object; the opponent is whatever the proof measures
against.

### Reagan's six shapes · emotional arc
**Use:** naming the curve a story already has, so the telling can pace to it.
**Shapes:** rags to riches · riches to rags · man in a hole · Icarus ·
Cinderella · Oedipus.
**Note:** this is a *vocabulary*, not a structure. It never overrides a row.

### Save the Cat · Blake Snyder
**Use:** production. Fifteen beats with page numbers.
**Why here:** it is a checkable list, which is what makes it a tool rather than
a theory. Read the board sideways and it is a beat sheet.

### Hero's Journey · Campbell / Vogler
**Use:** film, and any audience that already knows it.
**Caution:** it works and it is everywhere, which is why it reads as generic.
Prefer the Story Circle (same shape, eight words, loops) unless the teller
asked for this one by name.

## The old copywriting mechanics

Not frameworks — moves. Usable inside any row, in commercial register only.

- **Problem · agitate · solve** and **before · after · bridge** — the two
  shortest ways to put a Knock and a Turn in one paragraph.
- **The slippery slide** (Sugarman) — the only job of the first sentence is to
  get the second one read.
- **"So what?"** (Halbert) — the reader is always asking it. Answer it in the
  next line or cut the line.
- **Hook · story · offer** (Brunson) — earn attention, tell the story that makes
  the offer make sense, then make the offer. Never reorder these.
- **The epiphany bridge** — the moment the teller realised something, and the
  reader is invited to realise it too. This is Aristotle's *recognition*, sold
  by the unit.

## Evidence is a story too

- A **testimonial** is a Labov narrative in one paragraph: who I was, what went
  wrong, what happened, what changed.
- A **case study** is a hero's journey with numbers.
- A **proof block** is the payoff of the promise the headline made.

Every kept promise is a testimonial the world wrote. Render it as one.

## The finding underneath all of it

Transportation (Green & Brock): a reader absorbed in a story lowers their guard,
and beliefs change through story that would never change through argument.

Marketing took that as licence. **Take it as responsibility.** The refusals in
`SKILL.md` are the reason this registry is safe to hand to a machine.
