# Regulatory Landmines - Mover

## Federal framework (US)

- **USDOT Number** - required for any mover operating a commercial vehicle in interstate commerce. Issued by FMCSA. Must be displayed on truck + website + estimates.
- **MC Number (Motor Carrier Operating Authority)** - required to transport household goods across state lines for compensation. Separate from USDOT. MC-HHG designation.
- **FMCSA 49 CFR Parts 370-379** - household goods transportation rules. Governs estimate types, Bill of Lading, inventory, weighing, delivery windows, claims process.
- **"Your Rights and Responsibilities When You Move" booklet** - FMCSA requires interstate movers to provide this to every customer BEFORE the move.
- **Bill of Lading** - required contract for every interstate household goods move. Customer signs at pickup AND delivery.
- **FMCSA SAFER lookup** - public database. Reviewers + regulators check carrier status here. A "NOT AUTHORIZED" status on an MC number is a hard stop for interstate work.

## Estimate types (FMCSA-defined - language must be exact)

- **Binding estimate** - price is locked. Mover cannot charge more even if shipment is heavier than estimated. Customer cannot pay less.
- **Non-binding estimate** - mover estimates, final price based on actual weight + services. Mover cannot collect more than 110% of non-binding estimate at delivery (the "110% rule"). Balance due in 30 days.
- **Binding not-to-exceed (guaranteed not-to-exceed)** - customer pays the lower of the estimate OR actual weight-based cost. Customer-friendly. Increasingly the industry standard.
- All estimates must be in writing, signed, and dated. Verbal-only quotes are a regulatory red flag.

## Banned / high-risk claims

- **"Guaranteed pickup date"** - FMCSA and state regulators take hard line. Movers give pickup + delivery WINDOWS, not guaranteed dates. Violation risks cargo-claim dispute + BBB complaints.
- **"Lowest price guaranteed"** - classic bait-and-switch scammer language. BBB + FTC flag.
- **"No hidden fees"** without a published fee list - meaningless and legally risky. If used, must be backed by transparent pricing page.
- **"100% damage-free move"** - cannot guarantee. Creates contract liability that undercuts released-value default.
- **"FREE move" / "Free first hour"** - avoid "FREE" caps; can be used modestly ("complimentary 1st-hour labor with 4-hr booking") with disclosure. "FREE" is also a spam trigger per agency Rule 07.
- **"Licensed and bonded" without specifying** - must specify what license (USDOT, MC, state intrastate) and what bond (cargo, passenger, surety).
- **"Fully insured"** alone - must specify coverage type (cargo, general liability, workers comp). "Full Value Protection" is a specific FMCSA term, not a general insurance claim.
- **"We waive your deductible"** - not applicable the same way as roofing, but any implied absorption of claim deductible on damage claims should be reviewed.
- **"BBB A+ rated"** - only if currently accredited and currently A+. Check BBB profile before publishing.
- **"USDOT approved" / "FMCSA endorsed"** - FMCSA does not endorse movers. It registers them. Wording matters.
- **"AMSA certified"** - AMSA was folded into ATA's Moving & Storage Conference. The cert is **ProMover**. "AMSA Certified" is outdated / misleading.

## Deposit, prepayment, and payment red flags

- **FMCSA guidance:** reputable interstate movers do NOT require large deposits. Any deposit demanded before pickup is a red flag in customer research. If used, must be fully refundable within a clear cancellation window.
- **Cash-only or wire-transfer demands** - FMCSA lists this as a consumer red flag. Mover sites should not advertise "cash only."
- **Prepayment in full before pickup** - regulatory risk + scam signal. Interstate: balance is due at delivery (or within 30 days on non-binding estimate per the 110% rule).
- **State caps on deposits** - some states (CA, NY, others) cap % or require refundability. [VERIFY current state-by-state]

## COI (Certificate of Insurance) requirements

- Most apartment complexes + commercial buildings require a COI before move-in / move-out. Typical limits: $1M general liability, $1M auto, workers comp statutory.
- COI must name the building / management company as additional insured.
- Movers should offer "COI available on request - 24-48 hour turnaround" as a trust signal.
- Do not claim COI coverage limits that are not actually on the policy — the building mgmt will verify with the insurer.

## Hostage-loading and "rogue mover" pattern (what customers fear)

Customer research shows this is the #1 fear. Sites should address it head-on:
- Quote is low to win the job
- Mover loads truck, then "reweighs" and demands 2-3x quoted amount
- Refuses to unload until paid in full
- FMCSA has formal complaint process (filecomplaint.fmcsa.dot.gov)
- Protection: binding or not-to-exceed estimate, verified USDOT/MC, avoid any mover that won't do an in-home or video estimate
- Best mover sites publish this warning + how they differ

## Required disclaimers

- **Estimate type** (binding / non-binding / not-to-exceed) must be stated in every written quote
- **Released value liability** default is $0.60/lb federal minimum - customer must be offered Full Value Protection as alternative, with price
- **Delivery spread** on long-distance (typically 1-21 days depending on distance) must be disclosed in writing
- **Cancellation policy** must be on site or in estimate
- **Payment methods accepted** and timing (deposit vs balance) must be disclosed
- **USDOT + MC number** on every interstate estimate, ad, vehicle, and web page
- **State intrastate license number** on every intrastate estimate + website, where required (CA, FL, TX, NY, etc.)

## Examples by jurisdiction

- **US interstate:** USDOT + MC-HHG required. 110% rule on non-binding estimates. Your Rights booklet required.
- **California:** Cal-T number, CPUC jurisdiction, Max Rate Tariff 4, 3-day cancellation.
- **Florida:** IM number via FDACS. Annual registration.
- **Texas:** TxDMV Motor Carrier Registration. No separate consumer-protection regime like CA.
- **New York:** NYSDOT T-number + NYC DCWP license in NYC.

## When to flag for legal review

- Any "guarantee" language - pickup, delivery, price, damage, satisfaction
- Any "lowest price" claim
- Any claim about insurance coverage amounts or types
- "FREE" in any headline or CTA
- Deposit / prepayment language above state caps
- Any claim of federal / state endorsement
- Cross-state marketing where intrastate license is needed per state
- Customer testimonials that make specific damage / timing claims mover cannot substantiate
